- `<AsciiNoise>`'s field, warp, tunings and ramps are after `ascii_noise_bg`, a Processing sketch shared with the owner (2026-11-20); its lattice hash is Dave Hoskins' [Hash without Sine](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/4djSRW) (MIT, © 2014 David Hoskins).
- `<DitherGradient>`, `<TruchetField>`, `<PixelHorizon>` and `<FlutedGlow>` are new constructions after reference images the owner collected (2026-11-26): no code or artwork is taken from them. Truchet's tiles are Sébastien Truchet's (1704), in Cyril Stanley Smith's quarter-arc form.
- `<FluidFolds>`, `<FlutedBokeh>` and `<DitherGradient>`'s orb, pill and three-colour ramp are new constructions after the owner's Fluid, Fluted Glass and Dithered Gradient backgrounds (2026-11-28): no code or artwork is taken from them.
- `<PaintDrip>` and `<PhotoBokeh>` are new constructions after the owner's references (a spray-stencil badge with drips, a dahlia photo in shallow focus; 2026-11-28): no code or artwork is taken from them.
- `<GlassDrop>` and `<GlassStack>` are our own constructions after the ideas of reactdrops.dev's [Liquid Glass Logo](https://reactdrops.dev/components/text-reveal/liquid-glass-logo) and [Glass Slab Logo](https://reactdrops.dev/components/logo/glass-slab-logo) (2026-11-26). Their code is under the ReactDrops Component License 1.0, which forbids redistributing it in a component library, so none of it (code, shaders, presets or assets) was read into or copied here: GlassDrop is a height field over a signed distance (a circle, a rounded square or the logo's own field, mixed as the drop spreads), GlassStack a 2.5D stack of discs drawn in one pass from the logo's slices; both mirror the shared studio in `runtime/glass-optics`.
- `<WaterSurface>` is our own construction after a photo the owner sent of dusk water in shallow focus (2026-11-28); no shader code is taken from anywhere. Its techniques are published ones: waves summed from a spectrum with the deep-water dispersion relation, after Jerry Tessendorf's *Simulating Ocean Water* (SIGGRAPH course notes, 2001) and Mark Finch's *Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models* ([GPU Gems, ch. 1](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models)); waves filtered by the pixel's footprint and their lost slope carried into the glint's roughness, after Bruneton, Neyret and Holzschuch's [*Real-time Realistic Ocean Lighting using Seamless Transitions from Geometry to BRDF*](https://evasion.inrialpes.fr/Publications/2010/BNH10) (Eurographics 2010), here also widened by the lens's circle of confusion for the depth of field; Schlick's Fresnel approximation; a Beckmann highlight.
- Oklab is Björn Ottosson's.

The full notices are in [LICENSE](../LICENSE).
- `<Planet>`'s Earth is textured from NASA Visible Earth imagery, public domain: the Blue Marble (day) and the Black Marble city lights (2012/2016), with a water mask, resampled to 2048 × 1024 and 4096 × 2048 (taken from the copies in [three-globe](https://github.com/vasturiano/three-globe)'s examples). Its air follows the usual reading of Rayleigh and Mie scattering (a blue band along the limb, a forward lobe round the sun, Henyey–Greenstein); its flare is the lens-flare's.
